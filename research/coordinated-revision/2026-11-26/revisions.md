## Paper 1 — Architectural Economy v10

### Revised synthesis and scope
The original question remains reciprocal: how does the evolution of computing illuminate biological regulatory architecture, and how might biological observations inform future computing? The PDP-8 exemplifies continuity of a computational interface across physical implementations. The Hopper-inspired studies extend the investigation from sequential instruction execution to parallel logical workloads. They examine the same class of questions—resource reuse, dependency concentration, coordination costs and functional preservation—but do not emulate or measure the NVIDIA H100. The computational abstraction, physical implementation and biological mechanism must remain distinct.

The retained v9 study shows reuse savings and coordination liability in PDP-8 multiplication and Boolean-network simulations. Its K562 pilot does not support H2 (636 regions, 408 genes; adjusted reach coefficient −0.026, 95% CI −0.094 to +0.042). The companion reporter study gives partial molecular support for H3-M, not proof of conserved endogenous coordination or biological resource economy. These results are unchanged. Two additional CPU pilots now extend the fault scope and show why the topology of protective dependencies matters beyond the number of backup components. This is an exploratory extension of H1; no original experiment was rerun for this revision.

### 7.6 | Parallel Boolean dataflow: protection depends on fault type
H100 laboratory v0.1 evaluates 30 matched seeded portfolios at each of 8, 16 and 32 outputs, using all 4,096 states of 12 binary inputs. Four logically equivalent architectures (dedicated, shared, modular shared, buffered) passed all 360 portfolio-level equivalence checks. The study evaluated 32,168 modeled single-node faults under stuck-at-zero and inversion models. Output integrators and backup OR gates are explicit faultable sites. The equal-site blast metric is the number of outputs that differ from the golden mapping on at least one input, averaged within a design and across seeds. It is not a physical failure probability, latency, or per-input error rate.

At K=32, full sharing reduces mean node count from 96.0 to 47.9. Buffered motifs show a mean stuck-zero blast of 0.108, versus 0.941 for dedicated motifs; but including all internal sites raises buffered mean blast to 1.008, versus 0.941 for the dedicated design. Under inversion, buffered all-site mean blast reaches 2.395 versus 1.000 for dedicated. The protection is therefore fault-specific and sensitive to the coordination layer. These results extend the earlier synthetic Boolean benchmark to another fault model; they are not an independent demonstration of GPU reliability.

### 7.7 | Parallel matrix workload: protection depends on source independence
Hopper-inspired matrix pilot v0.2 uses 30 seeds, 64 products of 8×8 integer matrices per portfolio, eight groups of eight outputs, 16 staged A copies and 72 products for each of four logical designs. The reported 7,680 fault-free output checks and 33,000 modeled fault trials compare shared parity, separate-stage parity, separate-root parity and selective duplication. The count match does not establish equal area, energy, memory traffic, availability or source-acquisition cost. Independent root injection is an assumption of the model. Parity and algorithm-based fault tolerance (ABFT) are established methods, not claimed inventions.

When a staged primary A operand is corrupted, shared parity reports eight incorrect unflagged outputs, whereas separate-stage and separate-root parity flag the affected group (zero unflagged wrong). A common upstream primary-root corruption defeats separate-stage parity (64 unflagged wrong) but separate-root parity flags all 64. Detection is not correction: the wrong outputs remain present. Identical corruption of both roots defeats all four designs. Shared B corruption, opposing equal-magnitude compute errors, and corruption combined with an unavailable check also defeat relevant protections. A corrupt verification root can flag 64 correct outputs; router outage loses eight outputs for all four configurations. No fail-closed policy is implemented. Equal weighted means across unequally sampled fault families would be misleading, so outcomes are reported by event.

### 7.8 | Revised principle, hypotheses and return experiment
H1-D (prospective refinement): at fixed effective resource and verification budgets, reducing common-cause dependence between execution and checking should reduce undetected output disruption for nominated fault families. It need not do so under common upstream corruption, unavailable check paths, correlated errors, or excessive coordination cost. Compare full duplication, conventional ABFT (including weighted checksums), independent-source designs, and a biologically motivated compensatory design. Freeze workload, fault taxonomy, budget, metrics and holdout before evaluation. Report correct, wrong, unflagged wrong, flagged and missing outputs separately. Evaluate worst case and event strata; report cost vectors, not a single fabricated economy score.

H1 remains supported only in the specified models. H2 remains negative in the K562 pilot. H3-M remains partially supported by reported reporter-level evidence, with post-hoc motif results requiring confirmation. H3 remains untested at the endogenous system level. H1-D is a new prospective test, not a new biological finding. A logical return experiment has begun, but no engineering improvement attributable specifically to biological inspiration has been established. Actual H100 validation requires exact outputs first, then measured time, memory traffic, energy (if instrumented) and device-specific profiling. Record SKU, runtime, driver, clocks, precision, and uncertainty. A null result is informative. The broader conclusion is that architecture can be studied through changing implementations; protective dependencies must be represented explicitly, not counted as free independent backups.

## Paper 2 — Functional Equivalence Mapping v1.2

### Revised synthesis and scope
The retained v1.1 analysis compares enhancer function under sequence turnover, with assay-dependent equivalence estimates, a post-hoc JASPAR analysis and selected nonadditive mutation series. At the primary ±0.15 log2 margin, 244/386 active paired fragments (63.2%) met the empirical-Bayes equivalence criterion, versus 168/386 (43.5%) without moderation. The margin was changed after control-distribution inspection, and the original ±0.50 margin gave 99.2% equivalence. The reported post-hoc held-out AUC increase from 0.5971 to 0.6260 is modest and not a direct measure of binding; uncertainty and external replication remain necessary. The four selected series meeting the nominal two-sided 95% negative-nonadditivity criterion are not a population estimate. Prior work already addresses compensatory evolution. No new genomic dataset or statistical calculation has been performed for v1.2.

### 4.2 | A new biological question generated by the parallel-computation models
Paper 1's related parallel workload experiments distinguish the number of protective elements from their dependency topology. In the matrix model, a parity check sharing the corrupted upstream operand can agree with an incorrect result. Moving the check to a separately staged copy protects against local corruption but not common-root corruption. An independently injectable second root detects that modeled fault, while shared or identically corrupted sources remain problematic. The Boolean model similarly shows that the value of buffering depends on fault type and the faultability of coordinators. These are engineered logical examples, not evidence that enhancers implement checksums or that their dependencies behave like digital faults.

The original null findings for simple locus-level multiplicity remain unchanged (GSE113978 co-active tiles p=0.612; GSE180714 co-targeting HARs p=0.457). They address individual-element retention, not stability of endogenous gene expression under regulatory perturbation. A different hypothesis is now suggested: at matched enhancer count and baseline activity, the diversity of functionally supported upstream dependencies among co-active enhancers may predict target-output resilience after perturbation of a particular regulator. Two enhancers with shared dependencies may both respond to a single perturbation; distinct dependencies may provide alternative routes, subject to the target's integration logic. Shared chromatin context, target-promoter dependence and other common causes can defeat either arrangement. This hypothesis is about target-level perturbation response, not a retroactive replacement of the failed multiplicity prediction.

### 4.3 | Prospective dependency-overlap test (H3-D)
Unit: target gene × matched cell state × specified regulator perturbation; include validated enhancer-target links and at least two co-active enhancers. Select data suitable for endogenous responses, not reporter barcodes as independent observations. Use functionally supported regulator-dependence profiles, distinguishing evidence of perturbational necessity from motif/PWM proxies. For nonnegative prespecified profile weights a and b, define overlap as sum(min(a,b))/sum(max(a,b)); specify treatment of zero denominators and missing annotations in advance. Aggregate mean pairwise overlap over the enhancers of each target. Test whether lower overlap predicts smaller absolute log2 target-expression changes after the nominated perturbation, conditional on comparable baseline expression and perturbation strength. Prespecify a biologically meaningful margin, effect direction and uncertainty procedure before accessing outcomes.

Compare a baseline model containing enhancer number, activity, distance, target expression, regulator expression, cell state, and appropriate technical covariates with baseline plus dependency overlap and perturbation-specific interaction. Hold out genes or chromosomes, ideally a genuinely independent cohort. Cluster uncertainty by target and perturbation; account for multiple comparisons and nonindependence. Use shuffled dependency assignments preserving counts and target-degree structure and unrelated perturbations as controls. Alternatives include accessibility, annotation error, pleiotropy, shared chromatin and assay precision. Failure to improve out-of-sample prediction, or reproducible opposite direction, counts against the proposal. No fit-for-purpose perturbation dataset has yet been selected or verified; GSE110760 alone cannot identify endogenous causal dependencies. Expert review of the protocol is required before execution.

### Revised interpretation
The existing molecular equivalence results and their limits are retained. The new engineering studies contribute a question about shared regulatory dependency, not new support for molecular conservation, resource economy, or causality. The observed null multiplicity associations motivate a shift in explanatory variables and scale, not an assertion that redundancy is unimportant. This is a concrete instance of computing informing biology again; any biological confirmation must come from independent biological measurements. The new study is explicitly prospective, separate from the exploratory analysis already reported in v1.1.

## Paper 3 — The Augmented Investigator v0.5

### Revised abstract and case scope
The research program began with a reciprocal question: computer evolution may illuminate biological organization and biological observations may inform future computational architectures. Selected manuscript versions, dated dialogue and a public repository record trace a progression from PDP-8 architecture to molecular enhancer reanalysis, then back to computational experiments motivated by modern parallel AI accelerators. The new CPU pilots make the return direction operational without demonstrating actual H100 performance or novel bio-inspired engineering improvement. Their results also illuminate a methodological problem: multiple checks can share a dependency and therefore agree despite a common error. This analogy motivates explicit independence-of-verification requirements for AI-assisted inquiry. It does not measure model error correlation. The distinction among methodological reach, epistemic competence and evidential warrant remains central; the new work adds an observable case episode and a proposed verification-dependency gate. This retrospective single-investigator study does not establish a causal productivity gain, classroom efficacy, or independently validated biological discovery.

### 11.1 | The return direction becomes an experiment
After asking whether the same analytical class could be applied to NVIDIA's H100, the investigator requested an implementable lab. The repository contains two distinct CPU experiment series: Hopper matrix v0.1/v0.2 and the H100 Boolean-dataflow lab v0.1. Their code, results and protocols are inspectable. The Boolean lab reports 360 functional-equivalence checks and 32,168 modeled fault cases. The matrix v0.2 pilot reports 7,680 fault-free output checks and 33,000 fault trials. These counts indicate scope, not validity or productivity. The pilots were not independently reproduced for this manuscript revision. Actual CUDA execution on H100 hardware remains pending.

The case now records a progression from analogy to question to implementation to revised question, rather than only a proposed return journey. The mathematical examples show that protection can fail when data and checker share a corrupted dependency. Separating staged inputs detects some local failures; a separate logical source detects a broader modeled failure; identical corruptions can still defeat both. This supports a new research question in Paper 2 about diversity of regulatory dependencies. It does not show that the biological analogy produced a superior engineering design. The investigator's role includes maintaining the question, choosing when to pursue an extension, requesting preservation and directing revision. Model-specific attribution and full prompt/response coding remain incomplete.

### 12.1 | Add a verification-dependency gate
The six-stage inquiry cycle and the purpose and evidential gates in v0.4 are retained. Extend Stage 5: for every material claim, map the primary evidence, data version, transformations, assumptions, analysis code, reviewer identity or role, and outcome. Ask what single error could cause every apparent check to agree. A second AI using the same data, script, mistaken premise or generated explanation is not necessarily an independent evidential pathway. Even an independently coded computation may share an incorrect data definition. Independence is relative to the error being checked, not the number of tools, models or reviewers.

Record checks as proposed, executed, reproduced, externally reviewed, or unresolved. Distinguish agreement, correction, repeatability, independent reproduction and substantive disciplinary review. A successful checksum or matching hash establishes file identity, not scientific validity. For consequential claims, seek different error-detection paths: original-data reconstruction, separately specified implementation, negative controls, independent measurements and qualified specialist appraisal. Document what each path shares and what it can detect. Failed checks and dissent must be retained. Unresolved material concerns leave the claim provisional. This is a proposed operational framework; no evidence yet shows that it improves review outcomes.

### 13.1 | Educational evaluation and revised conclusion
A prospective study could use matched inquiry tasks with seeded errors and independent blinded scoring, counterbalancing single-AI review, multiple-AI shared-source review and independent-source review. Prespecify primary outcomes: material erroneous claims detected, valid claims incorrectly rejected, quality of explanation and calibration; secondary outcomes include time, cost and reviewer workload. Match tasks and resources, record the source of each error and the actual independence of the checks, obtain consent and appropriate ethics approval, and estimate inter-rater agreement. The framework is weakened if the independence gate fails to improve material error detection or creates excessive false rejection or burdens. No study of AI error correlation or classroom trials is reported here.

The central insight is not that computers and genomes are the same, but that their comparison can generate productive reciprocal questions. The new CPU experiments add a concrete return-direction episode and a useful analogy for epistemic responsibility. They leave scientific validation unresolved. Preserve the question without protecting the expected answer; verify the evidence without mistaking agreement for independence. The investigator remains responsible for understanding the purpose, seeking qualified help and limiting the claims. No AI system is an author; human approval and specialist review remain outstanding.
